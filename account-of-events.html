3.Account of Events

Important. Provide a clear factual record of what you saw, heard, or did. Stick to what you personally witnessed. Do not speculate or assign blame — that is the investigation's job, not yours. If you didn't see something, say so. If you're unsure, say so.
💡 Prompts — things to consider including (click to collapse) ▼

Your involvement. What were you doing at the time? What was your role on shift? Who were you working with? What work order, permit or task were you on?

What happened — in time order. What did you see, hear, or feel? What did you do? What did others around you do? Try to be specific about times if you remember them.

Conditions at the time. Things to think about:

  • Plant operating conditions, alarms, system warnings
  • Equipment condition, abnormalities you noticed before or after
  • Permits, isolations, lockouts in place (or not)
  • Lighting, weather, noise, time of day if relevant
  • Communications — radios working, instructions clear, language barriers

Immediate actions you took or witnessed. Emergency stops, isolations, evacuations, calls to control room, first aid, communications to other shifts.

Other people present. Only mention people if you actually saw them or interacted with them. Don't speculate about who might have been there.

Anything else relevant. Photos taken, calls made, documents referenced, training you've had on this kind of task, anything unusual that day, near misses you'd noticed before.

Reminder: If you don't know something, say "I don't know". If you didn't see something, say "I didn't see this". A truthful "I don't know" is more useful than a guess.
