6.Event Details

What Happened?

Provide a factual chronological narrative. Include: when (date, time, shift), where (specific plant area, equipment tag), who (people involved, including third parties), what activity was being undertaken (work order, permit number), and the sequence of events. Describe both the actual harm and any consequences. Avoid root cause language at this stage — that comes later. Stick to what was observed and recorded.

What Failed?

Identify the immediate failure modes — the proximate causes that allowed the event to occur. Examples: equipment failure (hose burst, valve seized), procedural failure (permit not raised, isolation not verified), human factors (lapse, slip, miscommunication), control system failure (alarm bypassed, interlock defeated). Reference any specific procedures, equipment tags, or safety controls that did not function as intended. Do not yet ask "why" — that is the next section.

Why Did it Happen?

Explore the underlying conditions and contributing factors. Consider: human factors (training, competence, fatigue, time pressure, distractions), workplace factors (lighting, weather, ergonomics, access), organisational factors (procedure clarity, supervision, communication, change management), equipment factors (design, age, maintenance regime, suitability for task), and performance influencing factors (workload, novel/unusual tasks, shift handover). For Level 2 events, the formal RCA section below will explore root causes more rigorously.

What Immediate Corrective Measures Were Taken?

Describe actions taken on the day to make the situation safe and prevent immediate reoccurrence — for example: emergency stop activated, area cordoned off, system isolated, permit suspended, equipment quarantined, alternative procedure invoked, communication issued to other shifts. Include who took the action and when. This is not the place to record longer-term recommendations — those go in Section 6 (or in the RCA below for Level 2 events).
